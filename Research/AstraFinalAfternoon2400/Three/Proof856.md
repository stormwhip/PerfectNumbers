Frozen main `495c59f55456a70994ce7848487e0c4e262c1984`. Let `N` be odd perfect, `N≤B=10^2400`, `3^856||N`, and `7,11,13,19,31∤N`. Let `X=3^856`, `W=3X`, `A=(W−1)/2`. This note treats the historical E3 domain: the unique Euler prime `q` has exponent one, `q∤A`, and `s=v3(q+1)>0`. It requires no complete factorization of `A` or proof of existence of a hypothetical survivor. **If an ordinary emitter of `q` lies outside `A`, then `s≥696`; if one lies inside `A`, then `s≥678`.** The two source roles need not be mutually exclusive if more than one ordinary component emits `q`; either bound holds for any emitter of that role. Neither role is exhausted.

## Primitive sources and factor range

For each ordinary component `t^h`, `v3(σ(t^h))` is zero unless `t≡1 mod3`, in which case LTE identifies it with `v3(h+1)`. Every positive ordinary supplier prime is at least 43; the only allowed primes below that in its residue class are excluded by `7|σ(t^2)`. If it supplies `k` units, it incurs at least the first `k` marginal factors in `t^(3^k−1)`. Form a global tariff `M[j]` from the `j` smallest possible marginal factors, allowing all lower tiers regardless of compatibility with each other. Explicitly include every relevant prime `t≤15000` and all successive marginal factors below `15000²`, stopping at a forbidden divisor of `σ(t^(3^k−1))`, which persists at all higher levels; append `856` phantom entries of value `15000²`. This truncation lowers the price, never raises it. Any subset of ordinary components supplying `j` units has product at least `M[j]`.

Every possible emitter `r^f` must be primitive: `ord_q(r)=f+1`. Here is the proof implemented independently in [Root856.py](Root856.py), also explained by the frozen [SourceSquare](../../Session20261126/Source/SourceSquare.md). The price `X·M[856−s]` excludes `s≤8`. For `s≥9`, `q≥Q=2·3^s−1>f+1`; order 1 is impossible, by LTE. If the odd order is proper it has index at least 3 in `f+1`. Since `q` divides the sum for the exact order, `r^f>r²(2Q/3)^3`. Let `b=v3(σ(r^f))≤min(6,856−s)`; a seventh unit is impossible by `43^(3^7−1)>B`. Bound `r^f` below by the integer `F` equal to the maximum of the rounded-down imprimitive bound and `43^(3^b−1)` (the latter is 1 for `b=0`). Two independent lower bounds are `X Q F M[856−s−b]` and `X F²/3^b`. For the second, `σ(r^f)/3^b` divides `N` and is coprime to `3r`, so `X r^f σ(r^f)/3^b` divides `N`. Their maximum is strictly greater than `B` in every (s,b) cell. Thus `f+1|q−1`, `3∤f+1` and this emitter supplies **no** 3-units.

Every prime divisor `u` of `A=Φ857(3)` has exact order 857 for 3: 857 is prime, `u=857` is ruled out by direct division, and `u` cannot divide `3(3−1)`. Hence `u≡1 mod1714`. Since its order is odd, 3 is a quadratic residue mod `u`, so also `u≡±1 mod12`. [Range.cpp](Range.cpp) sieves every `u=1+1714k` through `k=58400000` by a complete finite sieve (preserve a candidate equal to the prime doing the sieving); all remaining integers of the necessary mod-12 classes, whether prime or composite, must fail `3^857≡1 mod u`. The largest inspected cutoff is `100097600001`, so all prime factors of `A` are at least `R=100097600002`. We verify `A` is a nonsquare; all primes of `A` are ordinary, so the product of their actual prime powers in `N` is at least `A·R` (some odd multiplicity in `A` needs another copy). No assertion of squarefreeness is used.

## Complete split on source role and incoming 3-units

Write `q=2·3^s m−1` with `m` odd, coprime to 3. Both classes `m≡1,2 mod3` are included and the lower bound `q0` in each class is found by scanning at most 28 positive representatives. A skipped representative either forces a canonically absent prime in `m` (which divides `(q+1)/2`), or is a multiple of a smaller prime in the fixed trial set. At the 28th end one retains the lower endpoint for the full remaining class. Nothing assumes that the first surviving candidate is prime. Put `Y=3^s`, `k=856−s`, and let `i` be the number of these `k` ordinary units coming from the `A` support; the chosen emitter supplies zero. All `i=0,…,k` are inspected. The disjoint non-emitter outside ordinary powers have product at least `M[k−i]`. The supplying powers on `A` support alone have product at least `R^(2i)`, since `3^j−1≥2j` for `j` units. Write `J=max(AR,R^(2i))`; never multiply these two lower bounds for the same support.

* Exterior emitter: powers on `A`, the source, the root, Euler, and the outside ordinary suppliers are all disjoint. As `r^f>2q/3`, get `N>(2/3)X q² M[k−i] J`.
* Interior emitter: put `c=v_r(A)`, `t=r^(f−c)`. The balance inequality below gives `t>Yq/[max(W,q)(W/Y+4)]=H`. The `A`-support costs at least `J`, `A H`, `(2q/3)R^(2i)`, and `H R^(2i)` independently; the last two use other source primes disjoint from `r`. Multiply only the maximum of these by the **disjoint** `X q M[k−i]`. The comparisons are monotone in actual `q` (including `q/max(W,q)`), so `q0` is safe. Additionally `N>(2/3)X q² M[k]` independently by ignoring the overlap with `A` altogether.
* Independent divisor-sum bound: an ordinary component with `j` units emits the exact integer factors `Φ_(3^a)(r)/3` for `a=1,…,j`. Each factor is coprime to 3 (LTE), and every prime divisor has odd 3-power order for its base, so is `1 mod3`; in particular no such factor is divisible by `q≡2 mod3`. Multiply all selected factors with `A` inside the product of actual divisor sums: **their product** divides `σ(N)` even when anonymous supports overlap. Multiply by `X` and `q` only because this product is coprime to them. Thus `N≥X A q T[k]`, where `T` is a tariff of the same truncated smallest-factor type applied to these geometric-sum factors. No extra parity or source charge is added to this bound.

For completeness, the balance inequality is the specialization to center 3 of the frozen [balanced emitter derivation](../../Night2400/B/HardAtomRoute.md#L27-L36). With `u=(W−1)/r^c`, `w=σ(r^f)/q`, `δ=W/Y`, both `u w(r−1)−u−rt` and `u(w+σ(r^(c−1)))−σ(r^(f−c))` are divisible by `Y`. The first is nonzero since modulo the even `r−1` it equals `−W`. The second is nonzero since, after multiplication by `r^c`, it becomes `W(w+σ(r^(c−1)))−(q+1)w`, whose second term has 3-valuation at most 856 by `σ(N)=2N`; the first is divisible by `3^857`. Write `J0=max(1,W/q)`. If `r−1<δ+2`, oddness gives `r≤δ+2`. The first integer equals `((W−1)/q−1)rt−u(1+1/q)`; when positive it is at most `J0 rt`, and when negative its magnitude is less than `(2J0+r)t` using `u≤u w<(3/2)J0 t`. Otherwise the second integer, when positive, is less than `(3/2)J0t+W/(r−1)` with `W/(r−1)≤Yδ/(δ+2)`, and when negative has magnitude less than `3t/2` by the geometric sum upper estimate. In all cases magnitude ≥Y forces `t>Y/[J0(δ+4)]` (strictly, the limiting equality in the first positive case is already avoided by `r≤δ+2`).
