# Exact square-kernel bounds, independent of factorization

For a prime index `d` and integer base `p`, let `D=Φ_d(p)=(p^d−1)/(p−1)` and write `D=K g²` with `K` its positive squarefree kernel. Here `d∤D` and every prime divisor of `D` has order `d` for `p`, so each is `1 mod 2d`: if its residue of `p` had order one it would have to divide `d`; a prime dividing `p` cannot divide `D`. Therefore `K≡1 mod2d`. For the tested odd blocks `g²≡1 mod8`, so `K≡D mod8`. The combined unique positive progression has modulus `8d`, since `d` is odd. All its representatives through a declared endpoint are inspected. For any wheel prime `w`, discard a progression class only if **no** residue `z mod w` can satisfy `K z²≡D mod w`; this includes the cases where either residue is non-invertible. The remaining bounded representatives must fail either `K|D` or the exact check that `D/K` is a square. This argument addresses **all** possible squarefree kernels under the cap, not just prime or probable-prime labels. No absence of repeated prime factors is implied.

## Consequence under fixed Euler at `(3,12)`

When `3^12·797161·398581^108` is the exact prefix with fixed Euler `797161` to exponent one, every factor of `D=Φ109(398581)` is ordinary. The mass on its support is at least `D·K`. Put `p=398581` and let `b` be the sum of the incoming `p`-supply from the actual ordinary components on this support. The existing scalar price with exact remaining demand 107 gives `V=X p^214/U` for the full product; for the components **outside** `D` we can safely use their subsidy product bounded by the same `U`. Thus `N ≥ V·D·K/p^(2b)`. Since `K≥120000000001`, the integer verifier checks `V D K > B p^4` at the **lower** endpoint, so `b≥3`. This improves the historical two-unit lower bound; it neither assumes `D` squarefree nor closes the branch at exponent 108. The three incoming units need not be from distinct primes. [AuditKernel12](AuditKernel12.py) separately intersects a shorter CRT wheel in stock Python and checks the 84,858 remaining candidate integers directly by exact division and square root; independent replay passes without GMP or importing the C++ wheel.

As a separate restriction, for both exponents `h∈{102,108}`, let `r` be any ordinary prime divisor of `Φ_(h+1)(398581)` and suppose `r^c` divides that block. [InverseBoundary12.py](InverseBoundary12.py) verifies all reverse-CRT possibilities as in [Inverse150](Inverse150.md), now with `r≡1 mod 2(h+1)`, giving the same upper bounds for both: for `c=1,2,3,4,6,8`, the incoming `v_398581(σ(r^f))` is at most `54,36,27,23,18,13`, respectively. Because `5^(398581−1)>B` there is no index LTE shift. These caps do not force all target supply to come from `D`, do not apply to Euler or exterior sources, and do not close either exact exponent.

[PairCap12.py](PairCap12.py) gives a combined necessary bound: any two distinct internal ordinary sources deliver at most 88 total Q-units at `h=102` or 90 at `h=108`. In fact two internal sources cannot both reach depth 35 (respectively 37). At a given depth `L`, each source has a nontrivial odd Hensel root modulo `p^L` and is `1 mod2(h+1)`. Their product lies in the same odd subgroup, potentially including the identity; the remaining cofactor `D/(r_1r_2)` is in the inverse product class and is also `1 mod2(h+1)`. The verifier takes the smallest complete source representative `U` and smallest complete cofactor representative `V` among these classes. Strict `U²V>D` excludes the joint allocation even if none of these minima correspond to actual divisors. The separate 54-unit bound is recomputed and adds safely to `L−1`. Since 88<101 and 90<107, the total required supply cannot be obtained solely from at most two internal sources. With exterior supply allowed, no closure follows. Independent [AuditPairCap12](AuditPairCap12.py), using repeated powering to lift instead of the candidate Newton/Hensel recurrence, exits 0 on both capacities.
