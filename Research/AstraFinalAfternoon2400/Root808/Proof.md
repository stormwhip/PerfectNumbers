# A factorization-free Euler window cut at canonical (7,808)

**Scope.** Let \(N\le 10^{2400}\) be odd perfect with \(7^{808}\parallel N\) and \(19\nmid N\). Write \(q^a\) for its Euler component. Then \(v_7(q+1)\le707\). The claim is one conditional exclusion, not closure of the key.

Put \(X=7^{808}, W=7X, A=(W-1)/6, B=10^{2400}, s=v_7(q+1), Y=7^s\). If \(s>0\), then \(q\nmid A\): otherwise \(7\) has odd order modulo \(q\), hence is a quadratic residue there, but \(q\equiv1\pmod4\) and \(q\equiv-1\pmod7\) contradict reciprocity. Also \(s\le808\) by exact valuation of \(\sigma(N)=2N\), since \(q+1\mid\sigma(q^a)\). Each prime divisor \(z\) of \(A\) has order 809 for 7 modulo \(z\) (809 prime, and \(A\) is coprime to 6 and 7), so \(z\equiv1\pmod{1618}\); verify \(z>10^7\) by modular powering of *all* those candidates up to \(10^7\), without a primality filter. No multiplicity or full factorization is assumed.

For every \(s=708,\ldots,808\), \(Q_0=2Y-1\le q\) and \(XAQ_0^5>B\), so \(a=1\). As \(q\nmid A\), at least one other ordinary component \(z^h\) emits \(q\). \(\sigma(z^h)<3z^h/2\), so \(z^h>2q/3\); the inequality \(2XAQ_0^2>3B\) forbids an exterior emitter (if \(z\nmid A\), the product \(XAqz^h\mid N\)). Hence take \(c=v_z(A)\ge1\). \(h\ge c\) is even. Because \(q>5031\), \(z\equiv1\pmod q\) cannot emit, nor can \(z=q\); the nontrivial odd order \(o\mid h+1\) must be \(h+1\) itself. Otherwise \(h+1\ge3o\) and \(z^h>(2q/3)^3\) from \(q\mid\sigma(z^{o-1})\), contradicting \(XQ_0(2Q_0)^3>27B\). Now \(h+1\mid q-1\), \(7\nmid h+1\), and \(q=2mY-1\), where \(m\) is odd, coprime to 7 and 19, and \(mY\equiv1\pmod{h+1}\). Absence of 19 from \(N\) justifies filtering it from \(m\) because \(q+1\mid\sigma(N)\). Advancing up to 80 proved-impossible terms in this progression gives a lower endpoint \(Q\le q\) (each prime trial divisor is smaller than \(q\)). The next untested term is also safe.

Use \(T=\lfloor B/(XAQ)\rfloor\). \(XAqz^{h-c}\mid N\), so \(z^{h-c}\le T\) and \(z^c\le A\). Scanning positive even \(h\) while \(XQ_0(10^7)^h\le B\) covers the tail; a higher power would contradict \(Xqz^h\mid N\). For each \(h,c\) the intervals use \(z^h>2Q/3\) as a lower bound and both of the above power constraints as upper bounds. The integer-root calculation returns floors, including at perfect powers. When \(c=h\), write \(A=u z^h,\ \sigma(z^h)=qk,\ M=6uk\). Then \(6u\le M<9A/Q\) and \((M-1)z\equiv M+6u\pmod Y\), by multiplying \(qk(z-1)=z^{h+1}-1\) by \(6u\) and using \(6uz^h\equiv-1\pmod Y\). Thus \(6u(M+6u)^h+(M-1)^h\) is a positive multiple of \(Y\). It is at most \(M(2M)^h+M^h\), tested at the exact largest permitted integer \(M\). No assumption that \(u\) is prime is needed.

For the remaining shapes use \(V=(W-1)/z^c\), \(k=\sigma(z^h)/q\), \(d=h+1-c\), and the same signed identities as the prior [906 analysis](../../FinalNight2400/SparseSeven/AllSupport.md). The two integers
\[
D_0=Vk(z-1)-z^d-V,\qquad D_1=V(k+\sigma(z^{c-1}))-\sigma(z^{h-c})
\]
are divisible by \(Y\) from their defining identities. If \(D_0=0\), then \(V\mid z^d\), impossible as \(6\mid V\) and \(z\) is odd. Thus \(D_0\ne0\) even when \(A\) is a prime power. Also \(D_1\ne0\): from \(z^cD_1=W(k+\sigma(z^{c-1}))-(q+1)k\), vanishing implies at least 809 units of 7 contributed by the distinct Euler and emitter components, contrary to 808. The upper bound for positive \(D_1\) is \(3WT/(2Q)+W/(z-1)\); for negative \(D_1\), \(-D_1<3T/2\). Thus if \(3WT<YQ\) and \(3T<2Y\), one must have \(z\le2W/Y+1\). The endpoint is an integer since \(Y\mid W\).

Finally \(D_0=((W-1)/q-1)z^d-V(1+1/q)\). With verified bounds \(l\le z\le H,\ V\le6\lfloor A/l^c\rfloor\), the positive side is strictly less than \((W-1)H^d/Q\); the negative magnitude is at most \(H^d+(1+1/Q)6\lfloor A/l^c\rfloor\). Also \(D_0\equiv-1-6A\pmod{1618}\) using \(z\equiv1\). As \(Y\) is invertible modulo 1618, the least **nonzero** magnitudes of either sign for \(D_0/Y\) in that congruence class can be compared against these integer floors. A final budget test comes from \(qz^{h-c}\le\lfloor B/(XA)\rfloor\) and \(z^h>2q/3\), yielding \(q^{2h-c}\le\lfloor B/(XA)\rfloor^h(3/2)^{h-c}\). These inequalities concern one emitter and do not multiply two bills for the same mass.
