# Three forced distinct factors if the anonymous (7,808) block is squarefree

Let `N≤10^2400` be odd perfect, `19∤N` and `7^808∥N`. Set `X=7^808`, `A=Φ_809(7)=σ(7^808)`. **If A is squarefree, then A has at least three distinct prime factors.** Neither squarefreeness nor the exact factorization of A is assumed as fact, and this condition does not close the key.

## Complete high-depth divisor exclusion

Because 809 is prime and `gcd(A,42·809)=1`, every prime divisor of A has order 809 for 7 and is `1 mod 1618`. Its ordinary supply to 7 can come from order one or three. At order three the base depth `z` is `v7(r²+r+1)`. We first show no prime divisor r of A has order three and `z≥227`. This implies if a true such r is `≤sqrt(A)` it lies in one of two CRT classes mod `M=1618·7^227`, whose residues reduce to the two nontrivial cubic Hensel roots. Otherwise the integer cofactor `A/r` is `≤sqrt(A)` and lies in the two inverse CRT classes. A base-2 failed Fermat congruence proves A composite, so the cofactor is >1 even when considering a single factor candidate. The calculation recomputes all four classes and explicitly rejects every least representative by divisibility.

[The lattice packet](Lattice1-227.json.gz) is a set of integer polynomial coefficient vectors, not an assumed factorization. The [independent arithmetic verifier](VerifyLattice.py) covers all intervals from the smallest positive class representative through `floor(sqrt(A))`, with contiguous endpoints and a witness pair in every relevant residue class on each interval. If a true divisor in a tested interval is `d=r+Mk`, then `d|(k+c)` for `c≡r M⁻¹ mod A`, since `d|A` and `gcd(A,M)=1`. Each reconstructed basis polynomial `A^(t−j)(k+c)^j` and its allowed monomial multiple is divisible by `d^t` at that integer k (`j≤t`). The combined polynomial norm is strictly less than `lo^t≤d^t` on every integer `0≤k≤floor((hi−r)/M)`, so the polynomial must vanish. The same is independently true of the second witness there. Modulo the certified prime 65537 their gcd is a nonzero constant, excluding a common integer root, regardless of any degree drop. The 147 intervals and 588 witness pairs are exhaustive by mechanical recomputation. No LLL algorithm or discovery library is used to verify this packet. The square-divisor multiplicity-two experiment was incomplete and is **not** a dependency.

## Integer mass contradiction for at most two factors

From `A³≢1 mod49`, at least one A-prime has base-depth less than two (or is order one). Otherwise their cubes and hence the cube of A would be 1 mod49. By LTE, ordinary positive contributions at an A-prime come from order one lengths, or cubic roots plus at most four units from the index, since `7^5>5031`. For a higher Euler exponent attached to an A-prime, reciprocity implies its base is a quadratic residue mod 7 (`ord_q(7)=809` odd and `q≡1 mod4`); thus its order mod 7 is again 1 or 3 and the same caps hold. Total internal ordinary or high-Euler supply from at most two A-primes is therefore at most `5+230=235`.

Use the independent standard-library price in [the arithmetic verifier](VerifySquarefree.py) and [finite ordinary/high Euler allowance](VerifyFactorCount.py), with weight 2 on the exact 7-flow. A strictly losing ordinary cubic base has `r²<7^(2z)`, hence `r<7^z`; all actual candidates are exactly the least lifted residue in the nontrivial cubic class. Then only the maximum loss per base is charged, with total `U<579125`; Fermat witnesses can safely remove composites among prime bases. Order-one length and LTE increments pay at least their value by `3^6≥7²`. For high Euler the possible orders are 1,2,3,6; the base pays against five exponent units. A shortfall would imply `q<7^z`, so the complete lifted least roots under the Euler congruence are all rechecked, and higher LTE units fit by the exact length inequality. No unreported high-weight assumption is used.

* If Euler is high, squarefree A is fully paid with at least exponent 2 at each A-prime (at an internal high Euler the exponent is ≥5). Factor out `X A²`; for the distinct off-A suppliers pay for at least `808−235` residual units. The exact inequality exceeds `B`.
* If Euler is linear and external, the whole A support is ordinary and pays `A²`; the Euler prime pays `q≥max(5,2·7^s−1)` for `s=v7(q+1)`. Exhaust all `0≤s≤808` and the price on at least `max(0,808−235−s)` exterior units. Every strict integer comparison beats `B`.
* If Euler is linear and internal, its order mod 7 is odd by the same reciprocity; `s=0`. All ordinary atoms give a lower bound `X q 7^(2·808)/U` on N. Separately squarefree A gives a precise mass lower bound `X A²/q`, and the other internal prime provides at most 230 units, leaving an independent off-A price `7^(2(808−230))/U`. Multiply these two inequalities only to bound `N²`. The exact product exceeds `B²`.
