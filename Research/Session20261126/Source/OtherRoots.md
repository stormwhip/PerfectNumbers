# Nonprimitive-source exclusion for five large-root families

Let S=(19,7,11,31,13,3,5,17), p be the canonical root, p^e||N, A=σ(p^e), N≤10^2400 odd perfect. This note only concerns an Euler prime q with exponent one, q∤A and s=v_p(q+1)>0; for p=7,11,19,31, q∤A follows anyway from quadratic reciprocity (q=1 mod4, q=−1 mod p but p has odd order mod q if q|A). No such shortcut is asserted for 13. At least one ordinary r^f||N emits q (q|σ(r^f)), f even, r≠p,q. Let b=v_p(σ(r^f))≤e−s; b is **not** generally v_p(f+1) when p>3.

**Test.** Let Q=2p^s−1>B.bit_length()+1, so q≥Q>f+1. Thus r≠1 mod q and d=ord_q(r)>1 divides f+1. For odd L≥3, if (f+1)/d≥L, then q|σ(r^(d−1))<3r^(d−1)/2 and hence r^f>r^(L−1)(2Q/3)^L. In particular let F=floor(3^(L−1)(2Q/3)^L). The following independent bounds must hold:

1. N≥p^e Q F because the root, source, and Euler prime are distinct;
2. N≥p^(e−b) F² because σ(r^f)/p^b is odd, coprime to pr, and divides N.

If r∤A both bounds gain a factor A: in (1) A is coprime to pqr, and in (2) A·σ(r^f)|2N by multiplicativity, not by assuming the two geometric sums are coprime. Use p^(e−b)≥p^s to obtain source-b-independent conditions that increase monotonically in s. Sieve L=3 (no imprimitive source), L=5 (index≤3), and L=p (p∤f+1 because d|q−1 and p∤q−1). Root bans do not enter the new constants. Positive b can still arise from ord_p(r)>1.

[OtherRoots.py](OtherRoots.py) enumerates all frozen e for these five roots. [OtherRoots.json](OtherRoots.json) records the s threshold in each monotone test (not a claim that the specified window was previously open). For (7,976), (11,676), (13,738), (19,660), (31,466), the L=3 all-source thresholds are 406,329,308,268,230 and the external-r thresholds are 222,233,170,139,164 respectively. There are 136 distinct (p,e) with at least one nonempty conditional cut. First-branch and sum-product volume estimates are consistent with the historical MiddaySupply proof (especially line 9); what is added here is the **generalized missing order index**, and the following two-sided consequence, not a claim to have invented the root-source volume lemma.

**Second order.** In the primitive-q range let b>0. Because p∤f+1, necessarily r≠1 mod p (by LTE), and o=ord_p(r)>1 divides f+1. With p∤f+1 the exact identity v_p(σ(r^(o−1)))=b follows by LTE on (r^o)^((f+1)/o)−1. If o<f+1, then its odd index is ≥3, and r^f>r²(2p^b/3)^3. Independently p^b q divides σ(r^f), so r^f>2p^b Q/3. Use the stronger F of both bounds in (1),(2), now with exact b. When this stronger bound exceeds B and the preceding simple one did not, the source must have o=f+1. Hence f+1 divides the odd part of p−1 and simultaneously divides q−1. [DualOrder.py](DualOrder.py) enumerates finite (s,b) cells; the two versions of its classes (all r and outside A) overlap and should not be summed. This can turn unknown factoring into finite fixed-degree emitter tests in the appropriate windows, but does not eliminate the b=0 family. Run both programs with `python -I -S -B` and optional checkout path. No complete undecided exponent is credited by this work.
