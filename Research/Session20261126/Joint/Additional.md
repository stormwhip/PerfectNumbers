2. **Root-factor range.** Every prime t|A has ord_t(3)=1163. Thus t=1+2326k and 3 is a quadratic residue modulo t, implying t≡±1 mod12. `Trial.cpp` sieves all candidates k≤65536·70000 by small primes (preserving the candidate if it equals the small prime itself); for *all* remaining residues in the valid mod12 classes, including composites, verify 3^1163≠1 mod t. Since q is outside A, the product of the actual ordinary components on primes dividing A is at least A times its squarefree kernel, and therefore exceeds AR. Independently this product is at least R^(2i) when these primes supply i units. Pay only the maximum, not the product, of these bounds. `Exact.py` therefore uses 2·3^e·q²·81^(e−s−i)·max(AR,R^(2i))/3 for E3a excluding the auxiliary net costs.
3. **Euler prefixes.** q+1=2m·3^s, with m odd (Euler form), 3∤m and m shares no banned factor because m|(q+1)/2|N. `Exact.py` computes the entire low prefix by integer square root for each s. The least nontrivial residue raised to f exceeds cap. All cases of every low candidate are impossible, so E3a has base >10^2219, prior to charging auxiliary net costs.
