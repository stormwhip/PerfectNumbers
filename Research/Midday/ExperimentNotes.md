# Supplementary experiments (not certificate credit)

**Scope.** All results in this note are diagnostics on the 511 remaining `(p,e)` pairs of the pinned main. Neither a probable prime nor a floating objective was passed into the ledger as proof. The released evidence is the independent integer or directed-interval replay, not the discovery environment.

* **Literature screen.** [Acquaah–Konyagin and Zelinsky's two-largest bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11734) and [Bibby–Vyncke–Zelinsky's three-largest bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.09420) offer quick tests when several large forced primes are known. If `c>b>a` are any three distinct known prime divisors, then the sorted three largest primes dominate them coordinatewise. Compare `c^3` with `3B`, `b^5` with `2B`, `a^6` with `64B`, or `(abc)^5` with `(2B)^3`. These are **alternative** lower bounds on the same `N`, not factors to multiply by the forest bound. Screening all 511 primary divisor sums with observed factor hints found zero threshold crossings. The largest diagnostic was only about `10^1362.6` on `(7,562)`. This does not rule out applications to primes forced later in a tree.
* **Global graph relaxation.** For a finite set of explicitly labelled primes, one variable per allowed exact exponent, two high-power tails and a nonnegative excess variable suffice to enforce incoming valuation and single-sender support implications. A positive price for exceeding `e` on the primary root enforces a necessary upper bound on selected emissions. The bare fractional relaxation with 5 exact options per parity scored 1954 on `(7,786)` and 1475 on `(31,316)`; raising the cap to 25 yielded about 2312 and 2138. Both are below the fully certified forest bounds. On 147 low-exponent available graphs, a one-sided scalar excess price did not improve the ordinary bound on the tested grid. The root-five terminal uses an additional abundancy constraint, much more explicit support, and a separate exact dual; failure of this scalar control does not apply to it.
* **Private square completions without factoring.** For a selected emitting cell, take a nonsquare rough divisor `C` of the odd geometric sum, with all prime divisors at least `L`. Reserve the entire support of `C` exclusively for that cell; remove it from every other emitter's output and from the initial labelled support. Choose reserved blocks pairwise coprime. If the Euler prime is outside `C`, at least one prime with odd multiplicity must be topped up; this gives one multiplier of at least `L`. If `C≡3 (mod 4)`, even an Euler prime inside `C` cannot remove all such deficits: some `3 mod 4` prime occurs to odd order. Otherwise permit a single global coupon, shared with all forest Euler alternatives. In two discovery trials with `L=10001`, the floors improved from roughly 2251 to 2270 on `(19,62)` and 2208 to 2219 on `(31,136)`; this pilot alone does not close either pair. Both were subsequently addressed by other methods. In particular, do **not** multiply independent parity top-ups from overlapping unknown blocks.
* **Transfer without related primary indices.** A subtree can be transplanted whenever its root is forced in the target state; its parent map is then just a suggestion. Recompute every trigger, root quota, exclusion set and Euler branch at the target. The extracted subtree bank contained 2,513 different possible roots and 5,510 distinct proposals. This is not equivalent to lifting an old numerical floor when the cyclotomic indices are unrelated. Failed and overlapping proposals are not additional certificates.

Discovery scripts require optional local candidate data (`NightCache`, historical proposal trees), Python `flint`, and sometimes SciPy or PARI. A reader does not need to recover those inputs to replay a registered packet. Online queries were only sources of untrusted integer candidates; no paid factoring service was used. The historical Ochem factor snapshot has a pinned digest in the repository's [preparation script](../../Scripts/Run72hWSL.sh).
