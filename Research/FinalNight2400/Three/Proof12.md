# Eight stricter windows in the hard 66 path (partial)

Assume an odd perfect N≤B=10^2400 with 19,7,11,31,13 absent and exact components F=(3^12,797161^66,5897^6,42059143035592870990447^2,P^12), P=589657170962820100934139512775879733212083419. This is a subcase of a secondary fiber, not an entire primary fiber. Write X=∏F, S=σ(X), D=S/gcd(S,X), Q=797161, A=σ(P^12), T=σ(Q^66)/5897. Direct integer division shows D=AT, coprime to X, gcd(A,T)=1. Every divisor prime of A (respectively T) has order 13 (respectively 67) for its center: the prime divides the indicated geometric sum and not 2k(z−1)z. Trial division through all permissible classes to 20M shows every such prime is at least H=20000001. Both A,T are nonsquares.

The historical exact two-target exception pricing and internal emitter bound are [derived here](../../Night2400/B/HardAtomRoute.md); the checker executes the actual integer price, not a verdict lookup. The only cases left after that price have an exterior Euler prime e to exponent one, v_Q((e+1)/2)=i, v_P((e+1)/2)=j and e≥a=2Q^i P^j−1. Its emitter must lie in A or T since an exterior ordinary component emitting e costs ≥ceil(2a/3) on top of XDe. In each block the ordinary components must supply at least H extra parity mass (some odd valuation in that nonsquare block must increase). On a proposed emitter block b, replace its parity factor by the maximum of H, ceil(ceil(2a/3)/b), and the historical balanced emitter net bound. Multiply by the OTHER block's parity H only. The minimum over the two possible blocks is called net. Thus all of X·D·e·net is charged within support of XDe only. This last disjointness matters: a generic numerical lower bound on N cannot be multiplied this way.

**New independent factor.** Let O be the set of prime divisors of N outside XDe. Since D has at most n=⌊log_H D⌋ distinct prime factors, their combined final abundancy, regardless of final valuations, is at most (H/(H−1))^n. If O has at most two primes, the maximizers of their limiting abundancies cannot exceed the product of 5/4 and 17/16: 3 is fixed, and 7,11,13,19 are banned. In every priced window the verifier checks exactly S/X · (a+1)/a · (H/(H−1))^n · 5/4 · 17/16 < 2. Multiplicativity contradicts perfection. Thus there are at least three distinct primes in O, all ordinary and therefore squarefull. Their disjoint product is ≥(5·17·23)^2. We obtain N ≥ X·D·a·net·(5·17·23)^2. All comparisons are strict at rejection; equality is never ruled out.
