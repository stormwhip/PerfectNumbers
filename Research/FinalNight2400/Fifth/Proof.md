Use the [square-base construction](../Root7Joint/Proof.md) to get distinct atoms of positive even index h with b either an odd prime or the square of the Euler prime. They account for all incoming supply apart from q+1, uniformly in the exponent of the Euler component. Put L=h+1, so L≤8000<t. A base divisible by a small prime s cannot occur unless b=s or s²; the verifier applies exactly this test, retaining every possible Euler square.

We price both target primes at weight 19/5. Write u,v for incoming units. At p there are only the order-1 and order-5 supplies. For order 1 put d=p^u|L and z=0; d-1 powers suffice to pay p^(19u/5) since 3^(5(p-1))≥p^19. For order 5 put z=v_p(b^5-1), k=v_p(L), so u=z+k; the basic four powers are priced by CRT, and 10k further powers suffice for the LTE increment. Let p^k denote the length factor in this case, and use k=0 if there is no p-supply. For t, order 1 cannot supply; otherwise its odd order o divides (t-1)/4 and L, is at least 5, is coprime to p, and v=v_t(b^o-1).

**Budget cases.** If o is 5 or t has no supply, enumerate the length-5 basic CRT price for the active order-five coordinates only. For double order 5, L is divisible by 5 p^k, hence h−4≥5(p^k−1) pays k. For p-order 1 combined with t-order 5, L≥5d, paying 4 and d−1 separately. The single-coordinate cases are the same with unused budgets omitted. If o>5, charge t with o−5 powers, and use at most four more for the base-order portion p^z (when present); the sum is at most o−1. Higher p-supply pays from L≥o p^k (if p-order 1, replace p^k by d). If t supplies alone it may discard four powers. Thus the joint loss is bounded by one low-order factor per base and one high-order factor per base; multiplying two distinct allowed factors here is conservative and follows a disjoint allocation of exponents.

**Finite enumeration.** Low-order charge is the maximum of 1 and all (p^z t^v)^19/b^20 (modulus one for a missing coordinate). At modulus Z=p^z t^v, b≥Z cannot lose. Otherwise the least representatives are exhausted via primitive generator, simple-root Hensel lifting and CRT. For high o the charge is t^(19v)/b^(5(o−5)); o−5≥4, so again b≥t^v cannot lose. Large o is discarded when the entire capped demand costs no more than 3^(5(o−5)); for remaining o>100 the checker simply enumerates odd b below the proven size cutoff. Including nonprimitive roots in this latter enumeration only adds losses. Let U be the product of these two per-base loss tables. For **any subset of actual atoms** its size product raised to the fifth power times U is at least the product of its supplies raised to the nineteenth power. No assumption on the number of unknown primes.
