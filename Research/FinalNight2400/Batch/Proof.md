# A batch of 42 high fibers for canonical (11,396)

Let N be odd perfect, N≤10^2400=B, 19,7 absent and 11^396 exactly dividing N. Then t=2383 is forced and none of its exact exponents 150,152,...,232 can occur. This is conditional, not the full key. The hypotheses, including absence of 19,7, enter the first-domain enumeration. Set p=11, e=396, A=G(p,e)/t, C=G(t,f) stripped of p, X=p^e t^f, D=AC, c=e−v_p(G(t,f)). G is the geometric sum. Certify t|G(p,e) once; t≡3 mod 4, so f is even. The size lower bound p^e A t^f first exceeds B at 468. Enumerating all smaller values with parity, forbidden/surplus fixed factors, abundance and XD tests gives exactly f=2,4,...,232. Thus 42 of 116 first fibers are excluded here, leaving 74 unproved by this packet. No empirical primality/optimizer assumption.

Use [the coprime-length argument](../Disjoint/Proof.md) with *different* numbers: effective lengths for p have prime factors in {5,11}; those for t in {3,397,2383}. Their product divides the atom index, so the two base-price budgets fit. The weights are 4 and 2 (order-five and order-three minima), all factors and prime generators and Hensel depths checked in [Verify.py](Verify.py). The sum of odd LTE surcharge costs cannot create a loss because 3^(p−1)≥p^4 and 3^(t−1)≥t². With a single table U at the largest possible demands 396,231, allowing any greater compatible demand only weakens a bound for a smaller branch. Candidates include the extra Euler square as well as prime bases. Its valuation and cost come exactly from the even-index geometric factor at q² of sigma(q^a), as in the cited proof. For any selected subset of atoms, product of sizes times U dominates p^(4*supplied p-units) t^(2*supplied t-units). All comparisons rational or integer.

Put i=v_p(q+1), j=v_t(q+1), m=p^i t^j, Q=2m−1 and V=p^(4(c−i))t^(2(f−1−j)). Reciprocity for p,t both 3 mod 4 rules out q dividing its fixed sigma block whenever the corresponding i or j is positive. Thus if q|A then i=0 and m≤min(t^(f−1),(A+1)/2); if q|C then j=0 and m≤min(p^c,(C+1)/2); otherwise XDQ≤B implies m≤(floor(B/(XD))+1)/2. For each j under the maximum of these caps, maximize p^(3i)t^j by choosing the largest admissible i. The necessity NU≥XQV≥Xp^(4c)t^(2(f−1))/(p^(3i)t^j) often contradicts B for an entire f with no further tests.

If not, use the exact finite i,j grid. Test price first; show externality by reciprocity or by the unique cofactor progression mod 4m as in the cited proof, then try XDQ. An external q must be supplied by an ordinary row r^h, with target supply factors P=p^u,T=t^v. Set H=max(A,C) if coprime, else H=D. Costs give 3NH>2XDQ² PT and 3NU>2XQ²V/(P³T), using mandatory and price bounds **alternatively**, not multiplying overlapping costs. Since PT is integral, PT≤limit=floor(3BH/(2XDQ²)), hence P³T≤limit·min(p^(c−i),limit)^2 and also ≤p^(3(c−i))t^(f−1−j). The second bound with the lesser of these two caps eliminates the window. Every i,j in every claimed f is either priced or rejected by these complete outside/supplier inequalities. This is an integer relaxation of the two-dimensional discrete supplier domain; inability to close a different f with the same test is not a feasible perfect number.
