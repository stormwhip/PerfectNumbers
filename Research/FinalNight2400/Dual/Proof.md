# Two-target accounting for the prefix 19^432 || N, t^f || N

This is a conditional reduction for an odd perfect number N <= B=10^2400. It neither assumes a small-prime support mask nor closes the entire key. Let p=19 and t=11842464881317660217. The certificate below uses integer arithmetic only. In the following f is one of the even values explicitly certified by the verifier. Both displayed components are exact, not just divisors. **Result: f=20 and f=22 are both impossible.** Run `python -I -S -B Research/FinalNight2400/Dual/Verify.py` from repository root (do not use -O).

**Normal form.** Euler's theorem gives a unique q^a || N with q and a both 1 modulo 4. It is not p or t. Write a=4k+1; regard q^a as q times (q²)^(2k), and its sigma factor as (q+1) times the geometric sum of length 2k+1 in base q². All remaining unassigned components have ordinary prime bases and even exponents. The resulting geometric sums, including the extra one with odd composite base q², will be called atoms. They have pairwise distinct odd integer bases, not including q, and exponent zero is allowed for the extra atom. This is a multiplicative and supply-accounting identity; it does not identify a composite base with a new prime divisor.

For s in {p,t}, consider an atom of base r, length L=h+1 odd, and supply u=v_s((r^L-1)/(r-1)). For u>0 there is a divisor d_s of L that captures the whole supply: if r=1 mod s, set d_s=s^u by LTE. Otherwise put o=ord_s(r), z=v_s(r^o-1), k=v_s(L), d_s=o s^k. LTE gives u=z+k. For u=0 put d_s=1. Their prime supports are disjoint: odd prime divisors of p(p-1) are 3,19 and those of t(t-1) are 11,101,433,3077160600929,t. Consequently (d_p-1)+(d_t-1) <= d_p*d_t-1 <= h. This allocates non-overlapping *exponent* budgets even when one atom supplies both targets, unlike inferring a fractional allocation from a single-target bound.

**Price for one target.** The verifier computes a loss U_s as a product over distinct odd base labels r of the maximum of 1 and s^(w*u)/r^(o-1), considering primitive roots of order o>1 lifted modulo s^u for 1<=u<=c_s. Here c_s is the total demand after the two fixed components have been accounted for. It permits arbitrary odd bases, including composites. If o=1, r^(s^u-1) >= s^(w*u) since s^u-1 >= u(s-1) and 3^(s-1)>=s^w. For o>1 and L a multiple of s^k, the extra exponent cost is o(s^k-1)>=k(s-1); hence the additional supply costs no loss. For k=0 the criterion only needs testing at the least nonnegative representative of each root modulo s^u: larger representatives r>=s^u cannot lose since o-1>=w. The code constructs all primitive roots from a primitive generator modulo s and lifts each by the unique Hensel digit. For higher orders it alternatively checks odd bases directly below a strict cutoff. Each base occurs at most once among the atoms, so the product U=U_p*U_t suffices even for any subset of the atoms. For p=19 only, the implemented per-atom price uses 19^(2u-floor(u/20)) instead of 19^(2u), and U includes a compensating 19^floor(432/20): the sum of individual floor terms is at most that global floor. For the lifted part z+k the linear surcharge 2k still suffices, since floors only increase. This avoids multiplying unnecessarily gigantic fractions at borderline weight 2. For t the price weight is 5. Large orders are discarded only when 3^(o-1)>=s^(w*c_s). The order of the primitive generator for t is proved by the complete trial-certified factorization of t-1 and the Lucas criterion.

**Cover.** Put X=p^432*t^f, A=sigma(p^432)/t, C=sigma(t^f), D=AC. The program checks these are coprime blocks A,C with gcd(D,X)=1, v_t(sigma(p^432))=1, and the remaining demands c_p=432,c_t=f-1. Thus XD | N (using sigma(N)=2N). Write i=v_p(q+1), j=v_t(q+1), m=p^i t^j. Since q=1 mod 4 we have q=2m-1 mod 4m, hence q>=Q=2m-1. After q+1, atom demands are c_p-i,c_t-j. The first bound is N>=XQ*p^(w*(c_p-i))*t^(W*(c_t-j))/U.

If necessary rule out q in D. For Z=A or C, q|Z implies a positive integer l=Z/q <= Z/Q in the class Z/Q modulo 4m (division denotes multiplication by the modular inverse). Test the unique possible representative if Z/Q<4m, or discard Z<Q. Also, i>0 implies (p/q)=(q/p)=(-1/p)=-1 by quadratic reciprocity. Then q cannot divide sigma(p^432), since p^433=1 modulo q would imply p is a square in the group modulo q. Whenever these tests exclude q|D the second bound is N>=XDQ.

When q does not divide D, some ordinary even component r^h, distinct from p,t,q, supplies q: neither sigma(q^a) nor either fixed sigma supplies it. Write u,v for this component's two target supplies. Its sigma >= q*p^u*t^v since the primes are distinct; its sigma < (3/2)r^h. Since r^v_r(D)<=max(A,C), combining with XDq|N gives 3N > 2XDQ²*p^u*t^v/max(A,C). Alternatively applying the atom product bound only to the other atoms gives 3N > 2XQ²*p^u*t^v*p^(w*(c_p-i-u))*t^(W*(c_t-j-v))/U. For every v=0,...,c_t-j the former bound increases in u and the latter decreases. The verifier binary-searches the first u at which the former exceeds 3B (sentinel c_p-i+1 if none), then checks the latter at its preceding point if any. The supplier overlap can be reduced further: when u>2, write z=u-2. Since 3^(19³-1)>B, 19³ cannot divide h+1. The order-one case is then impossible by LTE; otherwise r mod 19^z is necessarily a nontrivial ninth root (the order is 3 or 9). For each Z in {A,C} separately, when m=19^z and m²>Z, check every nontrivial lifted ninth root R: the only r<m is R itself, and for r>=m dividing Z the cofactor k=Z/r satisfies 0<k<=Z/m<m and k=Z/R mod m. If no root admits either possibility, r cannot divide Z for this or any larger supply. Replace max(A,C) by the maximum of 1 and the blocks not excluded. Even if numerical upper bounds fluctuate, binary search returns either the sentinel or a tested threshold certifying the entire right interval; the decreasing second bound checks the left interval separately. Equality is never used as a contradiction. Every Euler exponent is covered by exactly the same i,j test, with no requirement that q^a have exponent one.
