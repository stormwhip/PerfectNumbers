# On the factorization of Φ907(7) in a hypothetical odd perfect number

**Conditional exclusion.** Suppose N≤10^2400 is odd perfect, v₇(N)=906, 19∤N, and A=Φ907(7) is squarefree. Then A has **at least five distinct prime factors**. No primality or squarefreeness is asserted about the currently unfactored 766-digit A. Rather, this rules out all squarefree factorizations with at most four primes, *for every Euler allocation*. In the high Euler or zero linear supply case, at most seven of its factors can be ordinary by [the upper-count theorem](Gain906.md). These bounds do not close the key.

Assume to the contrary that A has at most four distinct primes. All r|A are ≡1 mod1814. At least one has v₇(r³−1)≤1: otherwise r³≡1 mod49 at every divisor and A³≡1 mod49, contrary to A≡8 and A³≡22 mod49. The [simple-divisor exclusion](Divisor906.md) also gives v₇(r³−1)≤254 for order-three residues. For r≡1 mod7 only the length contributes, irrespective of v₇(r−1). Hence for ordinary components at these bases the total possible 7-supply is bounded by c=5+3·258=779: order-three LTE adds ≤4 length units and order one supplies ≤4. A low-depth r of some other order contributes zero and obeys the bound as well. The high-Euler case at a prime q|A causes no gap: ord_q(7)=907 is odd, so (7/q)=1, and q≡1 mod4 gives (q/7)=1; thus q is 1,2,4 modulo 7, of order 1 or 3. Whenever its sigma supplies 7 the same order/length estimate applies. The odd divisor lemma applies to residue depth, not only to the exponent being even. In particular the small-depth argument holds even if the special prime is one of the A factors.

Use the [subset valuation price](Gain906.md): any collection of ordinary components costs at least 7^(2u)/U for its total 7-supply u, U≤579125. A high Euler with a≥5 costs at least 7^(2u_q) including all its own geometric sum supply. Write X=7^906.

* **a≥5.** Each prime dividing squarefree A occurs to at least exponent 2 in N, even the Euler prime if internal. Thus A² divides N independently of the outside-A suppliers. Their total remaining demand is ≥906−779=127, including a high exterior Euler if applicable. Hence N≥XA²·7^254/U>B.
* **a=1 and q|A.** Its linear factor supplies no 7 by the same reciprocity. Global ordinary price gives N≥Xq·7^1812/U. Separately A²/q divides N (all other A-primes are ordinary); the at most three non-Euler A-primes supply ≤774. Therefore N≥X A²·7^(2(906−774))/(qU). Multiplying these inequalities bounds N², legitimately cancelling the unknown q. Exact arithmetic exceeds B².
* **a=1 and q∤A.** Now N≥X A² q·7^(2 max(0,906−s−779))/U, s=v₇(q+1). Write q=2m7^s−1 with m odd, gcd(m,7·19)=1. Enumerate s=0…906. The upper bound is ≤10 everywhere, so an admissible odd m is ≤9. The few q surviving the mass bound and allowed m are tested individually: every such q has a nonzero base-2 Fermat remainder and hence is composite. Thus no such Euler prime exists.

All thresholds are strict integer comparisons. [Cold verifier](Squarefree906.py) imports the generic price and the certified progression cap. It does not require an unproved ad hoc factorization, external queries or an LLL implementation. Removing the squarefree hypothesis remains necessary for an actual unconditional contradiction.
