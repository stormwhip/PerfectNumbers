# (7,906): strengthened factor-count upper guard

Let A=(7^907−1)/6 and 7^906∥N for odd perfect N≤10^2400 with 19 absent. Each distinct ordinary component r^h with r|A has a strict premium r^h/7^(2v₇(σ(r^h)))>10^14. Consequently **A has at most seven distinct ordinary prime factors** if the Euler exponent is ≥5 or its linear factor supplies no 7. This is an upper bound, **not** a contradiction: the historic upper count was 10 and there is no proof of a competing mandatory count of eight. Logically independent of the high-s Euler tail.

Every r|A is 1 mod1814. Let C=10^7. Direct product/gcd excludes every such divisor r≤C. For ordinary positive order-three supply write t=v₇(r³−1), j=v₇(h+1), h+1 divisible by 3·7^j; by the [depth255 certificate](Divisor906.md) t≤254. At each of these 254 levels and each cube root, enumerate all CRT representatives r≤C·7^t, testing their product modulo A. All 2,800,440 candidates are coprime to A, so r²/7^(2t)>10^14. Extra 3(7^j−1) powers of r pay 7^(2j). For order one write u=v₇(h+1): if u>0 then h≥7^u−1 and h−2≥4u, whence r^(h−2)≥7^(2u); for u=0 r² alone pays the premium. All ordinary cases are covered.

The usual square price for every other ordinary prime gives product cost at least 7^(2·ordinary supply)/U, ceil(U)=579125 as recomputed for this key with one maximum exception ratio per base. For a high Euler exponent a≥5, a≡1 mod4, the relevant nontrivial orders mod7 divide 6 and every such order divides 6. The least legal exponent ≥5 is thus 5. At exact base depth t test q^5≥7^(2t) on each primitive residue lift q<7^t; q≥7^t needs no check. For the length shift j, if a+1 includes 7^j and j>0 then for o=2,3,6, a−5≥o·7^j−6≥6j, paying the extra 7^(2j) with q≥3; order one likewise pays from a≥7^j−1. Thus high Euler incurs no further loss. For a=1 with zero 7-inflow no Euler deficit either. With k distinct ordinary prime divisors of A, the cost bound is N≥7^(3·906) C^(2k)/U; using k≥8 strictly exceeds 10^2400. No hidden factorization or squarefree assumption.
