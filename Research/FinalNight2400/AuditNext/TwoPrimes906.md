# If Φ907(7) has at most two prime divisors: stronger bounds on the Euler prime

For the same (7,906) setting as [the count lemma](FourFactors906.md), assume A has at most two distinct prime divisors, *without a squarefree assumption*. The high-Euler and linear zero-supply cases already contradict that lemma. If q^1 is linear with s>0, QR gives q∤A. **It is necessary that 442≤s≤710 and every ordinary prime supplying q belongs to A.** Thus merely one exterior ordinary source is impossible throughout the entire hypothetical two-prime branch, not just for s≥514. One of these at most two A-primes supplies at most 5 units of 7 (A³≢1 mod49). Call the other one's supply u (zero-pad if absent). In the low case u≤163. In the high case u=164…258, it occurs just once in A and r>10^7·7^(u−4), hence a further factor K=10^7·7^(u−4) is paid beyond A. In the low case take K=1. The missing outside supply after the linear prime and these two A-primes is at least v=max(0,906−s−5−u).

With Q=2·7^s−1, mandatory price N≥XAQK·7^(2v)/U. If an external emitter y^h emits q with j units of 7, y^h>2Q·7^j/3; paying all *other* outside components separately forces 3NU>2XAQ²K·7^(j+2max(0,v−j))≥2XAQ²K·7^v. These terms use disjoint mass and the same allowed global denominator U≤579125; K consists only of an extra copy of an A-divisor, never the exterior emitter. In either u-range the minimum of each bound is attained by the allowed u closest to 906−s−5: while v>0, each extra high unit costs 7 but saves 49 in the basic price or 7 in the emitter bound; once v=0 the bounds are nondecreasing. [Exact verifier](TwoPrimes906.py) checks both possible u categories over every 1≤s≤710, with the [previous high cap](Seven906.md). The first bound alone leaves 442…710; no window in that interval permits an exterior emitter. In particular this rules out the historic s=453 **exterior-emitter** scalar construction whenever A has at most two prime factors. This is not a determination of A's actual factor count or a full-key exclusion.
